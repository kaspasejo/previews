Viability deep dive

Competing with Footy Addicts

12 September 2026 · Prepared for Sam

Should you build a competitor to the UK’s casual-football platform? We pulled the real Companies House filings, the market data and the cold-start evidence. Verdict first, plan second.

Don’t build itAcquire it

A direct Footy Addicts competitor is not viable as an autonomous, ads-driven venture. The incumbent needed 13 years of manual community grind to reach roughly £111k/year profit with zero employees, and the thing that makes it work — local game liquidity built by organisers — cannot be bought with ads or scraped pitch data.

The scrape-pitches → SEO → player-profiles → organisers wedge attacks the wrong side of the market: pitch data is a commodity, and players do not create profiles where there are no games. The viable route in is the conversation you have already opened: acquire Footy Addicts. The filings show a profitable, growing, founder-dependent business with one decision-maker. If he won’t sell, the fallback is invest-and-operate, not compete.

The dual revenue model

  • Open platform: organisers post games free; players pay in-app; Footy Addicts pays organisers weekly minus 4.3% + 20p processing.
  • Own games: the company organises sessions itself with contracted “game facilitators”, bibs and balls — where the real margin sits. Player prices start at £2, typically ~£7 a session. The payment splits into VAT (20%), transaction fees, pitch hire (their biggest cost, up 55%+ in four years by their own account), facilitators, and reinvestment.

Funding: £349,800 of share premium on £1,200 share capital from 2018–19 allotments — roughly £351k of external money in. Tracxn lists one institutional investor, Niche Invest. Minority shareholders exist and would need buying out.

Reality-checking your numbers. Your “~£80k/year to the founder” is close to true — FY26 profit is ~£111k. Your “~£500k GMV” looks low: 6,000 games a month at even 8 paid players × £6 implies multi-million GMV, and £111k profit on £500k GMV would need an implausible 20%+ net margin. More likely GMV sits around £1.5–4M with the platform netting a few percent. Confirm with the founder’s actuals before any offer — but the filing trend (loss → £69k → £111k) says the business is real, growing, and accelerating since 2024.

Weaknesses users actually complain about

  • The Android app is poor: 2.88 stars from 345 ratings, ~71k lifetime downloads. (iOS is much better: 4.5 stars, 646 ratings.)
  • Refunds come back as wallet credit, not cash, with fees to withdraw — a recurring friction point in their own help docs.
  • Payment-sync bugs: their help centre carries a “Paid and not added in the list” article.
  • Light moderation: Trustpilot is 4.6 but only 86 reviews; complaints include rude, belittling players going unchecked.
  • London-heavy despite “140 locations”, and 13 years to reach this scale — organic-only growth, no marketing sophistication.
Section 2

The market is big, the commission pool isn’t

The FA’s State of the Game report counts ~1 million adults playing small-sided football at least weekly in England, with only ~218k in affiliated leagues — the large majority is recreational. The Football Foundation’s 2018 insight report adds the killer detail: 46% of small-sided players play for free, mostly in parks.

The money in this space lives in venues and leagues — Powerleague alone did £33.8m revenue in FY2016 across 48 centres — not in marketplace commissions. Footy Addicts’ own P&L (~£111k profit after 13 years) is the best empirical measure of the standalone ceiling for a game-organising platform.

Section 3

Who a new entrant actually fights

  • Footy Addicts: the incumbent network — community, organiser relationships, payout rails, a London FA endorsement.
  • Free substitutes: WhatsApp and Meetup groups and park kickabouts. The organiser’s default tool is WhatsApp, not an app — one Hyde Park Meetup group alone has 500 members playing free every Sunday.
  • Venue booking platforms: Playfinder, Bookteq, Schoolhire, Powerleague/Goals direct — they already own “book a pitch” search intent.
  • Adjacent apps: Sentr Football (2022, free) and Prematch, a German grassroots app expanding into England.
Section 4

Why the scrape–SEO wedge doesn’t work as drawn

  1. Pitch data is a commodity. Playfinder, Bookteq, Schoolhire, Powerleague and Google Maps already rank for “5-a-side pitch hire [town]”. Ranking a new directory takes years per county — and that traffic is low-intent for pickup games: people searching “book a pitch” already have a group. They’re organisers, not players looking for a game.
  2. Player profiles need games, not data. A player registers to join a specific game with a date, a time and an open spot. No games means no reason to register, so the directory converts to bounce. Supply has to come first.
  3. The marketplace is hyper-local. A game in Hackney does nothing for a player in Leeds. Every city is a fresh cold start — this is Andrew Chen’s Cold Start Problem structure, won city by city with manual hustle. Uber paid drivers, Reddit faked content, Airbnb scraped Craigslist demand. There is no national shortcut.
  4. The incumbent’s history is the warning. First game May 2010, founded 2013, app 2015, profitable only in FY2025. Gkortsilas seeded supply himself for years. Thirteen years to ~£111k profit.
  5. Organisers are the scarce side — and already served. Footy Addicts fills their spare spots and handles the money. There’s no pain big enough to make them switch, because their real alternative is their own WhatsApp group, which is free.

What would actually move organisers — including the ones you know personally — is guaranteed numbers: underwrite their empty spots (guaranteed minimum fill, or the platform pays the difference, or a guaranteed fee for the first ten games in a city). That is Chen’s “paying up for launch” play — it works, it costs ~£30–60 per underwritten game, and it is the opposite of autonomous.

Section 5

The economics of building

  • Unit economics: the platform nets roughly £0.50–£2 per booking after processing and VAT. An active casual player books ~2×/month — £12–48/year gross margin per active player, before churn.
  • Paid acquisition can’t carry that: UK sports CPC benchmarks sit around £3.56 in London, with fitness/leisure cost-per-lead at £17–24. Paying £15–25 to acquire a player worth £12–48/year, in a marketplace that has no games in their area yet, means payback beyond a year on users who mostly never activate. Ads work for reactivation once a city has liquidity — not for cold start.
  • Build cost is not the cost. You can build the platform yourself cheaply. The true cost is 2–4 years of city-by-city community operations — exactly the thing you want to be autonomous.
  • Realistic ceiling if built well: a second Footy Addicts in 5+ years, earning low six figures a year. Probability-weighted outcome: stalls at the empty-marketplace stage, as Sentr and others have.
Section 6

The viable play: acquisition

You have already spoken to the co-founder about an acquisition — that conversation is worth more than any build plan.

  • What you’d buy: ~£111k/year of growing profit, £329k of balance-sheet cash (price nets against it), a 210k community, organiser network, app, brand, payment rails and the London FA relationship.
  • Price logic: a lifestyle-business multiple on ~£70–111k of seller discretionary earnings — roughly 3–5× — puts the trade around £300–550k, adjusted for cash. Check the cap table early: minority shareholders from the £351k raise (Niche Invest et al.) need buying out, and the founder may want to keep The Grassroots Post separate — note the £28.5k interest-free loan from Footy Addicts to that company sitting on the balance sheet.
  • Why it fits you: your wedge ideas — SEO pitch data, automation, paid social, autonomous ops — are exactly what this business needs and lacks. It grew organically with zero marketing sophistication; a growth operator could plausibly 2–3× it. And you play on it twice a week — you are the user.
  • If the founder won’t sell: invest and operate rather than compete — buy a stake, take over growth and automation, leave him the community.
Section 7

If you build anyway, the only credible version

  1. One city, one niche Footy Addicts is weak in — your own London patch, women’s games, or over-35s, segments the Football Foundation report shows are underserved and respond to “an activator”.
  2. Seed supply manually: sign ten organisers you know and underwrite their games for 8–12 weeks.
  3. Keep the SEO pitch directory as a side-channel for organiser leads, not the engine.
  4. Only spend on ads once a city has weekly liquidity, aimed at reactivation.

Budget honestly: £20–50k in seeding subsidies plus 12–24 months before you know whether liquidity sticks. Autonomy comes after liquidity, never before.
