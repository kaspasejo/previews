Viability deep dive

Footy Addicts: build, buy, or walk away

12 September 2026 · Prepared for Sam · v2 with the founder’s actuals

The founder’s real numbers are in, he’s already quoted a price, and the business changed shape in January. Here is what the hard data says — verdict first, plan second.

Don’t build head‑onBuyout: keep warm, not nowNiche ops: optional

Don’t build a head-on competitor, and don’t chase the buyout at anywhere near his number. The founder asked ~£10M off future-value projections — that’s 25–90× what the business actually earns (£110k by the filings, up to ~£300k on the friendliest reading of his own sheet). Your own model values it at £2.8M, and even that leans on the new lean model holding up. A £10M anchor is not a negotiation, it’s a no.

The head-on build case got weaker, not stronger, in January: Footy Addicts slashed its take (from ~34% of player spend to ~12%) and cut staff and pitch costs to near zero — and profit went up. The “I’ll run it cheaper” wedge is gone; they already run it at zero headcount. The one thing they gave up is the facilitator-run game layer they used to operate themselves — that’s the only open lane, and it’s a hands-on operating business, not an autonomous one.

The move: keep the buyout warm — your £2.8M seller-financed model is documented, the FY26 filings make his £10M projection look sillier every year, and anchors crack. If you want in-market exposure now, the only grounded version is one city, one niche, facilitator-run games you seed yourself — treated as an operating business with a real time cost, never an autonomous one.

Section 2

The January pivot: they got leaner, not weaker

Your read on the voice note is confirmed by his own numbers. In the sheet’s Base Layer actuals, January 2025 is a hard break:

  • Take cut roughly in half: monthly platform turnover dropped from ~£90k (Nov–Dec 2024) to £39–61k — from ~34% of player spend to ~12%.
  • Costs gutted: salaries go to zero from January 2025, and pitch hire — previously £40–110k/month — effectively stops being a cost (months show net credits). Facilitator spend shrinks to a trickle. This is what “stopped organising their own games” looks like in a ledger: they exited the own-operated layer and became a pure booking platform.
  • Profit went up: post-pivot months run at £25–55k/month after tax in his sheet, and the FY26 filing (May 2025–Apr 2026, entirely post-pivot) confirms the new model works — +£111k retained, zero employees, £329k cash.

What this does to your case: two of the three build arguments die here. You can’t undercut them on take — they already cut theirs. You can’t out-lean them on cost — they run at zero headcount. What they left behind is the layer they abandoned: organised, facilitator-run games with quality control — the thing that made their sessions reliably better than a WhatsApp kickabout. That lane is open. It is also operationally heavy by definition — pitches booked 4–12 weeks ahead, facilitators on the ground, no autonomy.

Section 3

The market, honestly sized

~1 million adults play small-sided football weekly in England (FA State of the Game), but 46% of small-sided players play free, mostly in parks (Football Foundation 2018). The money pool concentrates in venues — Powerleague alone did £33.8M revenue across 48 centres — while the platform layer is a single low-six-figure-profit business after 13 years. Competition for a new entrant: Footy Addicts’ network, free WhatsApp/Meetup groups (the organiser’s real default), venue-booking platforms (Playfinder, Bookteq, Powerleague direct) for pitch SEO, and adjacent apps (Sentr, Prematch entering England).

Section 4

Why the scrape–SEO wedge still doesn’t work

  1. Pitch data is a commodity. Playfinder, Bookteq, Schoolhire, Powerleague and Google Maps already rank for pitch-hire searches — and that traffic is organisers who already have a group, not players looking for one.
  2. Players need games, not data. Profiles follow supply. No games means no reason to register; the directory converts to bounce.
  3. It’s hyper-local. Every city is a fresh cold start (Chen’s Cold Start Problem) — won with manual hustle, not automation. Uber paid drivers; Reddit faked content. There is no national shortcut.
  4. The incumbent’s history is the warning. First game 2010, founded 2013, profitable only from FY2023 — thirteen years of founder hustle, and he just proved the model only really pays when run lean.
  5. Post-pivot, the price war is unwinnable. At a 12% take with zero staff, there is no margin left to attack. The only unserved demand is quality-run games — an ops business, not a platform.
Section 5

The economics, with real inputs

  • Platform margin per booking is small: on his own FY24 numbers, £1.51M platform revenue on 49,635 games ≈ £30/game — before VAT, staff, development and pitch costs left the P&L at +£102k. The post-pivot lean version keeps ~£300–400k/yr by his sheet, ~£111k+ retained by the filings.
  • Paid acquisition can’t seed this: UK sports CPC ~£3.56 (London benchmark), CPL £17–24 — against pounds-per-year margin per active player, in a marketplace with no local games yet. Ads are for reactivation after liquidity, never for cold start.
  • The build cost is not the cost. 2–4 years of city-by-city community operations is — the exact thing you want autonomous.

Section 7

If you want in now: the only credible version

  1. One city, one niche the lean Footy Addicts no longer serves: quality-run games with facilitators — your own London patch, women’s games, or over-35s.
  2. Seed supply manually: ten organisers you know, games underwritten for 8–12 weeks.
  3. Charge for the quality layer, not the booking: curated games at a premium price point, run lean the way his own pivot proves possible.
  4. SEO pitch directory as a side-channel for organiser leads, not the engine. Ads only for reactivation once weekly liquidity exists.

Budget honestly: £20–50k in seeding plus 12–24 months of your attention. It could become a £50–150k/yr operating business. It will not be autonomous at the start, and it will not dent Footy Addicts — it lives in the lane they vacated.
