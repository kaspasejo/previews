The organising rule

Memory, ledger, output. Nothing overlaps.

One rule keeps the systems from stepping on each other. It is already written into Kai's design and it extends cleanly to the CRM's new home.

The vault is the memory

Who everyone is, and what was said.

kai-vault is the canonical record. Append-only history; profiles describe the present. If you ever leave any tool, you keep the history.

The CRM is the ledger

Pipeline state, money, outreach.

Deals, stages, sequences and subscription state live in admin.kaspasejo.com. Operational, fast, queryable. Everything written here mirrors back to the vault.

Content is the output

What the world sees.

content-automation turns the same voice and facts into posts, press and SEO. It reads the shared voice layer; it never invents its own.

Division of labour

Who does what - including me

Four workers, four jobs. The test for any task: which box does it belong in?

admin.kaspasejo.com Built, not deployed

Owns: the CRM + app admin
  • Contacts, pipelines, sequences and tracking - the sales machine's state
  • Adonae + Murmr admin: members, plans, promo codes, broadcasts
  • One audited record of every change, signed with your login email

Never: app-specific code (the hub contract forbids it), content, conversation.

Kai Live

Owns: the compounding private brain
  • Black book on everyone you actually deal with; meeting notes and follow-ups
  • Daily briefing, weekly review, hourly capture filing, @kai answers
  • Drafts in your voice, in Gmail, for your approval - never sends

Never: outbound without your approval, cold-prospect scraping into the black book, anything public-facing.

Content machine Running

Owns: the audience-facing output
  • Social, press and listening loops per brand, on routines
  • SEO autopilot across the six site repos
  • The canonical voice pack every other system borrows

Never: your relationships. It talks to audiences, not people.

Instinct Live

Owns: general assistant work across your life
  • Builds, research, errands, reviews - like this map, the HomeReady launch, the Play Console setup
  • Reads your repos, sheets and calendar; hands you drafts and previews to approve
  • Works across every brand on demand, no routines of its own in your repos

Never: replaces Kai. Kai is the brain you own outright; I am the extra pair of hands on everything else.

Your call, 11 September

What the admin-crm decision changes

You decided the CRM will be part of /admin-crm, hosted at admin.kaspasejo.com, alongside admin for Adonae and Murmr. That supersedes two older plans in the repos.

Before

CRM in two other places

Kai's config points at Attio (paid, per-seat) as the operational CRM, mirrored to the vault. The usm spec (issue #1, 10 Sep) planned a standalone CRM in the usm repo: Gmail extension plus a Fastify + Postgres server.

Now

One home: admin.kaspasejo.com

The CRM joins the app admin you already built. One sign-in, one audit trail, one Postgres behind the Worker, zero per-seat fees. Attio becomes optional history; the usm repo's Gmail-extension idea can live on later as a surface onto this CRM, or retire.

The path

From where things stand to the target

Grounded in admin-crm's "Where things stand" (11 Sep) and murmr/docs/GO-LIVE.md. Each phase is shippable on its own.

0

Today You are here

Kai is live on four routines and the content machine is running. The admin is built, tested and green on main in every repo - but nothing is deployed, and the CRM is still Attio lists plus the Master CRM sheet.

1

Switch on admin.kaspasejo.com

Dashboard work only, in order: Cloudflare Access application, deploy the Worker, set the secrets from the admin-crm README, run Adonae's hub.sql and Murmr's two migrations, set ADMIN_LIST_SECRET on the adonae-lander Worker.

  • Unlocks: live app admin for Adonae and Murmr behind one login
  • Same pass: Murmr's paid tier - Stripe product, RevenueCat project, Play products (step 2 of GO-LIVE)
2

Build the CRM inside admin-crm

Add the CRM next to the app admin: Postgres behind the same Worker, pipelines for Kulcho sales, Wick and Fundraising, one-shot import of the Master CRM sheet (~20k rows) and the Attio lists. The sheet retires to a read-only archive.

  • Unlocks: one contacts ledger across every brand, no per-seat fees
3

Repoint Kai from Attio to the new CRM

One config change in kai/config/config.yaml: the pipeline and sequence reads and writes move to admin-crm. The mirror rule moves with it - everything written to the CRM still lands in the vault. Sequences and outreach drafting start running against the ledger.

  • Unlocks: the sales machine runs out of your own stack, Attio can be cancelled
4

Always-on and clean-up

Move Kai's worker to a small always-on box (a planned config change, not a rewrite) so @kai answers in seconds and routines run 24/7. Once the admin has matched the in-app admin screens' numbers for a couple of weeks, delete those screens in Adonae and Murmr.

  • Unlocks: the finished shape - one brain, one ledger, one admin, always on
Found in the repos

Flags to resolve

Places where the repos disagree with the new shape. None are urgent; each is a small decision.

!

Kai still points at Attio

kai/config/config.yaml, master-spec.md and CLAUDE.md all name Attio as the CRM and sequence engine, with per-brand pipeline lists. That was true until today's decision.

Resolve in Phase 3: repoint the config to admin-crm, then Attio is optional.

!

The usm spec builds the CRM in the usm repo

usm issue #1 (spec v2, 10 Sep) planned a monorepo CRM: Gmail extension plus Fastify + Postgres server. Your admin-crm decision supersedes the server half.

Decide: does usm live on as a Gmail sidebar onto the admin-crm CRM, or retire?

!

shared-context is copies, not the submodule the docs claim

kai's docs describe shared-context/ as a git submodule shared with content-automation; .gitmodules is empty, so it is a plain folder of stamped copies. The voice files already drifted once under exactly this arrangement.

Decide: make it a real submodule, or formalise the copy-and-check dance.

!

Kai runs desktop-first

The desktop scheduled tasks, including the one-minute @kai poll, only run while your Mac is awake. The four cloud routines run regardless, capped at hourly.

Phase 4 fixes it: a small always-on VM, roughly £4-8 a month.

Decide: archive it so the admin-crm home is unambiguous.
